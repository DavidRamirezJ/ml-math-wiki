SVD, one step at a time

Start. Here's a unit circle of input vectors — every direction, all length 1. The two coloured arrows are reference axes we'll track. Press Next step ▶ to apply A one piece at a time.
What you're seeing. The matrix being applied is $$A = U\,\Sigma\,V^\top,\qquad \Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}3&0\\0&1.3\end{pmatrix}$$
  • Step 1 — Vᵀ rotate: spin the circle so its hidden "natural axes" line up with x and y. A circle looks identical, but the arrows turn — that alignment is the whole point.
  • Step 2 — Σ stretch: pull the x-axis by σ₁ = 3 and the y-axis by σ₂ = 1.3. Now it's a flat, axis-aligned ellipse. (This is the only step that changes size.)
  • Step 3 — U rotate: spin that flat ellipse to its final tilted angle. Done — that's exactly what A does.

Notice: stretch alone (step 2) can only make a flat ellipse. The two rotations are what let SVD tilt it — which is why every matrix needs all three pieces.
