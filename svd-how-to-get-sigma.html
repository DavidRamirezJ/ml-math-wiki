Where does σ₁ actually come from?

Two answers, same number: measure it off the ellipse, or compute it from $A^\top A$. Change A and watch.

1. The visual answer 👀

Apply A to the unit circle (gray). It becomes an ellipse (blue). σ₁ is just the length of the ellipse's longest radius. σ₂ is the shortest. Literally measure them.

input circle (radius 1) longest = σ₁ shortest = σ₂

So — what do you actually DO to get σ₁?

Three steps. Both panels above show the same σ₁ — the visual length and the formula always agree:

  1. Form the matrix $A^\top A$ (multiply A's transpose by A). It's always square & symmetric.
  2. Find its eigenvalues $\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2$ — the "natural stretch factors squared".
  3. Take square roots: $\;\sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i}$. The biggest is $\sigma_1$.

Why $A^\top A$ and a square root? Because stretching twice (once by $A^\top$, once by $A$) squares the stretch factor — so you undo it with a square root. The longest radius of the ellipse is $\sqrt{\lambda_1}$. Same number, two viewpoints.
