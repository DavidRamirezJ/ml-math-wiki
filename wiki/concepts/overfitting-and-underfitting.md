# Overfitting and Underfitting

> **One-liner:** Overfitting is memorizing the practice questions instead of learning the subject; underfitting is being too simple to learn it at all.

> See also the regression-specific treatment in [[overfitting]] (Ch. 9).

## 🟢 Intuition (Dummy)
You're learning to recognize dogs. **Underfitting:** your rule is "four legs ⇒ dog," so you call
cats and horses dogs too — too simple to capture reality. **Overfitting:** you memorize the exact
twelve dog photos you were shown, down to the background and lighting, so a *new* dog photo throws
you off — too obsessed with the specific examples. The sweet spot **fits well**: complex enough to
catch the real pattern, simple enough to handle new photos.

## 🟡 Mechanics (Practitioner)
Training fits a model class $M_{\boldsymbol\theta}$ to data drawn from an unknown true model $M^*$
(MML §8.3.3, Fig. 8.7). After optimizing to $\boldsymbol\theta^*$, three outcomes (Fig. 8.8):

| Case | Cause | Symptom |
|---|---|---|
| **Underfitting** | class too poor (too few params) | high training risk *and* high test risk |
| **Fitting well** | class just rich enough | low, similar training & test risk |
| **Overfitting** | class too rich (too many params) | low training risk, **high test risk** |

The diagnostic is the gap between **training risk** $R_{\text{emp}}(f,X_{\text{train}},\mathbf y_{\text{train}})$
and **test risk** $R_{\text{emp}}(f,X_{\text{test}},\mathbf y_{\text{test}})$, the latter being our
estimate of the expected risk $R_{\text{true}}(f)$. Overfitting is precisely when
$$ R_{\text{emp}}(f,X_{\text{train}},\mathbf y_{\text{train}})\ \ll\ R_{\text{emp}}(f,X_{\text{test}},\mathbf y_{\text{test}}) , $$
i.e. the training estimate badly **underestimates** the true risk.

**Worked example (Fig. 8.8).** Data generated by a roughly linear/cubic process. Fitting a degree-7
polynomial → overfit (wiggles through every noisy point). Fitting a flat line to sinusoidal data →
underfit. A modest-degree polynomial → fits well.

**ML usage.** Overfitting worsens with **little data + complex hypothesis class**. Fixes:
[[regularization]] / priors, more data, and detecting it via [[cross-validation]] (low train risk
but high validation risk).

## 🔴 Theory (Expert)
- Overfitting is the empirical face of the **generalization gap** $R_{\text{true}}-R_{\text{emp}}$ in
  statistical learning theory: capacity (VC dimension, Rademacher complexity) bounds the gap, and an
  over-rich class makes the bound vacuous even as $R_{\text{emp}}\to0$.
- It is the **bias–variance trade-off** in disguise: underfitting = high bias, overfitting = high
  variance. [[maximum-likelihood-estimation|MLE]] overfits in the small-data regime exactly because
  it has no variance control — adding a prior ([[maximum-a-posteriori-estimation|MAP]]) or a
  regularizer reins it in.
- [[bayesian-model-selection|Bayesian model selection]] handles it differently: the **marginal
  likelihood** marginalizes parameters out, so it is not prone to overfitting and embodies an
  automatic Occam's razor.
