# Augustin-Louis Cauchy (1789–1857)

> **Why they matter to ML:** The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality is what makes "the angle between two vectors" — and thus cosine similarity — well-defined.

## Life (brief)
French mathematician, one of the most prolific in history (~800 papers). A founder of rigorous
mathematical analysis, he put calculus on firm footing with precise definitions of limits,
continuity, and convergence. He worked across analysis, complex function theory, and mechanics.

## Key contributions
- **Cauchy–Schwarz inequality:** $|\langle x,y\rangle| \le \|x\|\,\|y\|$. Cauchy proved the
  finite-sum (vector) form (1821); Schwarz later gave the integral version. Today it guarantees
  $\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{\|x\|\|y\|}\in[-1,1]$, the foundation of the
  [[angle-between-vectors|angle]] and orthogonality machinery of [[inner-product|inner products]].
- Foundational work on **limits and convergence** (Cauchy sequences) underpinning real and
  functional analysis — the setting (Hilbert spaces) for inner products of functions.
