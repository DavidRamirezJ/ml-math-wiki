# William of Occam (c. 1287–1347)

> **Why they matter to ML:** his namesake principle — prefer the simplest explanation that fits — is the guiding intuition of **model selection**.

## Life (brief)
English Franciscan friar, logician, and philosopher. Best known for the methodological principle of
parsimony that bears his name (**Occam's razor**): entities should not be multiplied beyond
necessity, i.e. among competing explanations, prefer the simplest that accounts for the observations.

## Key contributions
- **Occam's razor** — in machine learning this becomes the rule that, all else equal, simpler models
  generalize better and should be preferred. It is the intuition behind
  [[bayesian-model-selection]], where the **marginal likelihood / evidence** enacts an *automatic*
  Occam's razor without any explicit complexity penalty. → [[bayesian-model-selection]]
