# Hermann Amandus Schwarz (1843–1921)

> **Why they matter to ML:** His half of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality extends the angle/similarity bound to function spaces — the setting kernels live in.

## Life (brief)
German mathematician, a leading figure in 19th-century analysis and a student of Weierstrass.
He worked on conformal mappings, minimal surfaces, and the calculus of variations, and succeeded
Weierstrass at the University of Berlin.

## Key contributions
- **Cauchy–Schwarz inequality (integral form):** Schwarz proved $|\langle u,v\rangle|\le\|u\|\,\|v\|$
  for inner products defined by integrals (1888), generalizing Cauchy's finite-sum version. This is
  what makes the [[inner-product|inner product of functions]] $\langle u,v\rangle=\int_a^b u v\,dx$
  yield well-defined angles and orthogonality — the basis of Fourier analysis and kernel methods.
- **Schwarz lemma** and contributions to complex analysis (not used in this chapter).
