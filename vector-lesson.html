🟢 WHAT IS A VECTOR

The two-question entrance exam

A vector is anything that passes both tests — no matter what it looks like:

CandidateAdd two & stay valid?Scale by a number & stay valid?Vector?

The property "operations keep you inside the club" is called closure. Looks (arrow? list? sound?) are irrelevant — only the two operations matter.

🔴 WHY ML NEEDS IT — the gradient-descent loop

The update rule that renders now:

$$\mathbf{w}_{\text{new}} \;=\; \mathbf{w}_{\text{old}} \;-\; \eta \,\nabla L$$

Read it: take the current parameter vector $\mathbf{w}_{\text{old}}$, scale the gradient $\nabla L$ by a small step $\eta$ (the learning rate), and subtract it. That is scale + add — the two vector operations — done in a loop. Closure is what guarantees $\mathbf{w}_{\text{new}}$ is still a usable parameter vector for the next step.

Below: a tiny loss bowl $L(w)=w^2$. Watch $w$ walk downhill. Each step is one application of the formula.

Try $\eta$ small (crawls), medium (smooth), and > 1.0 (overshoots & blows up — the "step too far" failure from the lesson).
